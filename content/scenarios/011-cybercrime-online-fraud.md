# Cybercrime and Online Fraud: How to Report and What to Do

> **If you have just lost money to an online fraud — call 1930 immediately.** This is the National Cyber Crime Helpline. Speed matters. The "Golden Hour" between fraud and reporting determines whether the destination bank account can be frozen before the money is moved out.

This page is **legal information**, not legal advice.

## The most important number: 1930

**1930** is the **National Cyber Crime Helpline**, operated by the **Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C)** under the Ministry of Home Affairs. It is **toll-free, 24×7**, and connects you to the Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System.

Calling 1930 immediately after a fraud:

1. registers the complaint in the I4C's central system;
2. flags the destination bank account so banks can attempt a freeze;
3. routes the complaint to your state's cyber cell automatically;
4. starts the formal record you'll need for any insurance claim, FIR, or recovery.

**The earlier you call, the better the recovery odds.** Government data reports that over ₹7,130 crore has been saved (frozen / recovered before withdrawal) through the system across 23 lakh+ complaints.

The portal at **`cybercrime.gov.in`** does the same thing online — file there if 1930 doesn't connect.

## What "cybercrime" covers

Cybercrime is any offence committed using a computer, internet, or digital device — or where one is the target. The most common kinds:

- **Online financial fraud** — UPI scams, card fraud, fake investment apps, bank impersonation calls, OTP scams.
- **Phishing** — emails or websites pretending to be your bank, government, or a service.
- **Identity theft** — misuse of your Aadhaar, PAN, or other ID.
- **Online impersonation / fake profiles** — someone pretending to be you on social media.
- **Cyberstalking and online harassment** — repeated unwelcome contact, threats, image-based abuse.
- **Hacking and unauthorised access** — to your accounts, devices, or networks.
- **"Digital arrest" scams** — see below; this has been the fastest-growing fraud type in 2024–2026.
- **Sextortion** — threats to publish intimate images for money.
- **Job and matrimonial frauds** — fake job offers, fake marriage profiles for money.

## What the law says

Cybercrime is prosecuted under **two parallel frameworks**:

### Information Technology Act, 2000

| Section | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **s.43** | Civil liability — damage to computer systems, unauthorised access, data extraction. Compensation up to crores. |
| **s.66** | Criminal version of s.43 acts done dishonestly or fraudulently — up to 3 years and ₹5 lakh. |
| **s.66C** | **Identity theft** — fraudulent use of someone's electronic signature, password, or unique identification feature — up to 3 years and ₹1 lakh. |
| **s.66D** | **Cheating by personation using computer resources** — pretending to be someone online to cheat — up to 3 years and ₹1 lakh. |
| **s.66E** | **Violation of privacy** — capturing, publishing, or transmitting images of a person's private area without consent — up to 3 years or ₹2 lakh. |
| **s.67 / 67A / 67B** | Publishing or transmitting **obscene material**, sexually explicit material, and material depicting children. Punishments escalate steeply. |
| **s.72** | Breach of confidentiality and privacy by someone with authorised access. |
| **s.75** | **Extra-territorial jurisdiction.** If a computer in India was involved, the IT Act applies even if the offender is abroad. |

### Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023

| Section | What it covers (with old IPC equivalent for context) |
|---|---|
| **BNS s.204** | Personating a public servant. (Formerly IPC s.170.) |
| **BNS s.205** | Wearing the dress / carrying token used by a public servant with fraudulent intent. |
| **BNS s.308** | **Extortion.** Forcing someone to deliver property by threat of injury — punishment up to 7 years and fine. |
| **BNS s.318** | **Cheating** and dishonestly inducing delivery of property. (Formerly IPC s.420.) Up to 7 years and fine. |
| **BNS s.319** | Cheating by personation — pretending to be someone else to cheat. |
| **BNS s.336** | **Forgery for the purpose of cheating** — including forged "warrants," "RBI letters," "court orders." (Formerly IPC s.468.) |
| **BNS s.351** | **Criminal intimidation.** Threats — including online — to cause hurt, property damage, or reputational harm. |

The **same act often violates several sections** — e.g. a digital arrest scam invokes BNS ss.204, 308, 318, 319, 336, 351 plus IT Act ss.66C and 66D. This is normal; the FIR should mention all applicable sections.

## What to do, in order, if you have been a victim

### Step 1: Stop the bleeding (first 60 minutes)

- **Call 1930.** This is the single most important action.
- **Call your bank's fraud line.** It is on the back of your card and on your bank's website. Ask them to **block the card / account / UPI** immediately and to flag the fraudulent transaction.
- **If a UPI fraud:** open your UPI app and freeze the account. NPCI provides a UPI fraud reporting flow inside most apps.
- **If credentials were compromised:** change every password the attacker may have learnt — banking, email, cloud accounts. Enable 2FA wherever possible.

### Step 2: Preserve evidence

- **Screenshots** of every conversation, email, message, fake document, transaction page.
- **Bank statements** showing the fraudulent transaction and the original credit (your money's path).
- **Call records** — note the numbers, time, and duration.
- **Original message bodies** (don't just screenshot WhatsApp — long-press → forward → email to yourself for the metadata).
- **The "Section 65B Certificate"** — under **BSA s.63(4)** (formerly Indian Evidence Act s.65B(4)), electronic evidence requires a certificate from the person controlling the device that produced it. Get one drafted by your IT department or a qualified person if you're going to court.

### Step 3: File the formal complaint at cybercrime.gov.in

Even after calling 1930, you should file a **detailed online complaint** at `cybercrime.gov.in`. The portal:
- gives you a **complaint reference number**;
- routes the case to the **state cyber cell** for action;
- can be tracked online.

You can complain anonymously for non-financial cyber harassment — but for financial fraud, your contact and bank details are needed for recovery.

### Step 4: File an FIR

The cybercrime portal is **not** a substitute for an FIR. For any criminal cybercrime, you should also **file an FIR** at your nearest police station — under **BNSS s.173** (see `/know/fir-process`). Mention:

- IT Act sections (66C / 66D / 67 / 43, as applicable);
- BNS sections (318 / 319 / 308 / 336 / 351 / 204, as applicable).

If your local police are unfamiliar with cyber matters, you can file at a **Cyber Police Station** (most cities have one). **Zero FIR** under BNSS s.173(1) means you can file at any station — useful when the fraud crossed jurisdictions.

### Step 5: Recovering frozen money — BNSS s.457

If 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in succeed in **freezing** the destination bank account, you don't automatically get the money back. To recover frozen funds, an application under **BNSS Section 457** must be filed in the **Magistrate's court** for **release of seized property**. The court, after notice to interested parties, orders release to the rightful owner.

This is a step where you typically need a lawyer. Most cyber crime victims don't know about it and assume the freeze is enough — it isn't.

## Specific scams: what to know, what to do

### "Digital arrest" scams

**There is no such thing as a digital arrest in Indian law.** Period. **CBI, ED, RBI, Customs, Supreme Court, no Indian agency conducts arrests over WhatsApp, Skype, or video call.** Real arrests are physical, in person, with a written memo.

The scam pattern:

1. A video call from "police / CBI / customs / court" claims a parcel in your name was stopped with drugs / fake passports / illegal currency.
2. They show forged warrants, fake ID cards, sometimes a fake "courtroom" backdrop.
3. They insist you stay on the call, threaten immediate arrest if you disconnect, and direct you to a "secure" account to "verify" funds.
4. Once money is transferred, the call is dropped and the accounts vanish.

**Your response:**
- **Disconnect the call.** Real police do not arrest by video.
- **Do not transfer money** "for verification" — that's never a real procedure.
- **Call 1930 immediately** if you've already paid.
- **File an FIR** under BNS ss.204, 308, 318, 319, 336, 351 + IT Act ss.66C, 66D.

### OTP / phishing scams

**Never share an OTP, PIN, CVV, or password** with anyone — not bank staff, not "RBI", not anyone. Banks and RBI never ask for these.

If you shared one:
1. Block the card / account immediately;
2. 1930;
3. Bank fraud line;
4. Cybercrime portal complaint.

### Image-based abuse / sextortion

This includes morphed images, leaked private images, and sextortion threats.

- Specific criminal sections: **IT Act s.66E** (privacy violation), **s.67/67A** (obscene material), **BNS s.79** (insult to modesty of woman), **BNS s.351** (criminal intimidation).
- Report to **cybercrime.gov.in** — the portal has a dedicated category, with **anonymous reporting allowed for women and children**.
- The **National Commission for Women helpline 7827170170** can support you through the process.
- **Stop**Tapped and **stoponlineabuse.in** (run by NGOs) can help with takedown requests to platforms.

### Online harassment / cyberstalking

- **BNS s.78** (stalking, including online stalking) is a specific offence.
- **BNS s.79** for word, gesture, or act intended to insult the modesty of a woman.
- Block, report on the platform, and file at cybercrime.gov.in. For sustained harassment, file an FIR.

### Cryptocurrency and fake investment scams

- The investment platform is a fake; the "broker" disappears once you try to withdraw.
- Same response: 1930, cybercrime.gov.in, FIR. Add **SEBI complaint at `scores.gov.in`** if it claimed to be a SEBI-registered entity.

## What law enforcement realistically can and cannot do

Be clear-eyed about outcomes. The conviction rate in cybercrime cases in India is approximately **1.6%** based on NCRB data (2020–2022). Most fraud rings operate from outside India or behind layers of mule accounts and shell SIMs. Money recovery is more achievable than offender prosecution — and **only if you act in the first hour or two**.

This is not a reason to skip reporting. Reporting:
- enables freezing;
- builds the data set that leads to platform-level interventions;
- preserves the record for any future civil or insurance claim;
- meets your responsibility if regulatory or company processes ask whether you reported.

## Bank-side liability

The **RBI's "Limited Liability of Customers" framework** (Customer Protection Circular, 2017) limits your liability for **unauthorised electronic transactions** if you report to your bank within prescribed time windows:

- **Reported within 3 working days:** zero customer liability (with limited exceptions).
- **Reported within 4–7 working days:** liability capped (varies by account type and amount).
- **Reported after 7 days:** the bank's policy governs.

This is a powerful framework that many victims don't invoke. **Tell your bank explicitly** that you are claiming relief under the **RBI Limited Liability Circular** and ask them to credit your account back, pending investigation. If they refuse, escalate to the **Banking Ombudsman**.

## Prevention — the 60-second checklist

- Never share OTPs, PINs, CVVs, or passwords with anyone, regardless of who they say they are.
- Don't click links in unsolicited messages — type URLs directly.
- Verify caller identity by calling back on a published official number, not the one they called from.
- Use **2FA** on email, banking, and social media.
- Keep your phone OS and apps updated.
- Use UPI's "set transaction limit" feature; lower the limit so a single fraudulent transaction can't drain you.
- Treat any urgency-based ask ("act in 5 minutes or you'll be arrested!") as a fraud signal.
