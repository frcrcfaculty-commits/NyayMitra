# Motor Accident Claims: Compensation, Insurance, and Process

If you, a family member, or a vehicle you own is involved in a motor accident, several legal regimes apply at once: **insurance**, **compensation through a Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT)**, and (where there's negligence) **criminal liability under the BNS**.

This is **legal information**, not legal advice. Engage a lawyer experienced in motor accident matters — these cases turn on documentation and timelines.

## What you should do immediately after an accident

### If anyone is injured

1. **Get medical help first.** Stop, render aid, call **108** (ambulance / emergency).
2. **Inform the police.** A "**Good Samaritan**" framework — formalised by the Supreme Court in *Savelife Foundation v. Union of India* (2016) and now in the **Good Samaritan Rules, 2020** — protects people who help victims from harassment by police, hospitals, or courts. You cannot be detained or compelled to be a witness as a Good Samaritan beyond a single statement.
3. **Hospitals must give immediate emergency treatment** — under MV Act s.161 and the Supreme Court's directions, **no hospital can refuse emergency motor-accident care** for want of payment or paperwork.
4. **Note vehicle numbers, witness contacts, photograph the scene.**

### If you are the driver

- **Stop the vehicle.** Failing to stop and report a fatal or serious-injury accident is itself an offence (BNS s.282 read with BNSS provisions on hit-and-run).
- **Inform the police** at the nearest police station within **24 hours**.
- **Inform your insurer** within the time prescribed by your policy (typically 24–72 hours).

## The criminal side, briefly

Under BNS:

- **BNS s.106(1)** — death by **rash or negligent act** not amounting to culpable homicide. Imprisonment up to **5 years and fine**. (Formerly IPC s.304A.)
- **BNS s.106(2)** — same but where the rash or negligent act causes death and **the person fails to report** the accident or **escapes from the scene** ("hit-and-run") — **up to 10 years**. This is a **new and stricter provision** introduced in BNS.
- **BNS s.281** — rash or negligent driving on a public way endangering human life. Up to **6 months and / or ₹1,000 fine**. (Formerly IPC s.279.)

Under BNSS, criminal proceedings begin with an FIR (see `/know/fir-process`). Compensation through MACT is **separate** from criminal liability — both can run in parallel.

## Compensation through the Motor Accident Claims Tribunal

MACT is the specialised forum for compensation. Set up under MV Act s.165, MACT applies its own rules of evidence, has wide discretion in fixing compensation, and is designed for victim relief.

### Two parallel routes for compensation

**(a) Section 140 — interim "no-fault" compensation (limited)**

For death or permanent disablement, **MV Act s.140** provides a **no-fault interim sum** — meaning you don't have to prove negligence. Amount is fixed: ₹50,000 for death, ₹25,000 for permanent disablement (these have not been substantially revised in many years and are usually superseded by the s.166 process).

**(b) Section 166 — full compensation on proof of negligence**

The substantive route. You file a claim petition before the MACT proving:

- the accident was caused by negligence of the driver / owner;
- the resulting injury / death;
- the loss suffered (medical bills, loss of income, future loss of earning, etc.).

There is no upper limit on compensation under s.166. The Supreme Court has developed a sophisticated jurisprudence around computing compensation, especially for **fatal accidents** (the *Sarla Verma* / *Pranay Sethi* methodology — multiplier method based on age and income).

### What you can claim under s.166

For death of the breadwinner:

- **Loss of dependency** — calculated as (deceased's annual income × multiplier × 2/3 for personal expenses, adjusted by family size). Multipliers range from 5 to 18 depending on age.
- **Future prospects** — the Supreme Court in *National Insurance Co. v. Pranay Sethi* (2017) held that an addition for future prospects must be made (40% if salaried with permanent job aged below 40; 25% for self-employed below 40; etc.).
- **Conventional heads** — funeral expenses, loss of consortium, loss of estate (currently ₹15,000–₹40,000 each per *Pranay Sethi*, periodically revised).
- **Medical expenses** incurred before death.

For injury:

- **Medical expenses** (past and likely future).
- **Loss of income** during treatment / recovery.
- **Loss of future earning capacity** if there's permanent disability.
- **Pain and suffering, loss of amenities of life.**
- **Cost of attendant / nursing care.**

### The 2019 amendment — Section 164 (interim relief expanded)

The MV (Amendment) Act 2019 inserted a new **s.164** — providing **₹5 lakh for death and ₹2.5 lakh for grievous hurt** as **interim relief on a no-fault basis**, replacing the old s.140 quantum for many practical purposes. This is a significant and often-overlooked entitlement.

## The detailed report system — Section 158(6) and Section 159

Under MV Act s.159 (as inserted/amended in 2019), the police **must, upon registering an FIR for a motor accident, file a Detailed Accident Report** with the MACT **within 90 days**. The DAR triggers the MACT's process **even if the victim hasn't filed a separate claim**.

This is meant to give victims a default path even when they don't know to file a claim. Make sure the police register the FIR; that pulls the rest of the process behind it.

## Time limits

**MV Act s.166(3)** required claim petitions to be filed within **6 months** of the accident, but this provision was **omitted** by the 1994 amendment, then partially revived in the 2019 amendment, which provides for a **6-month limitation** from the date of the accident — though this has been judicially read as directory in deserving cases. Timely filing is strongly advised; delays beyond 6 months should be supported by an application for condonation with reasons.

## Insurance — third-party vs own-damage

Every vehicle in India must have at least **third-party insurance** (MV Act s.146). This is a statutory minimum — you cannot drive without it, and driving without it is a punishable offence.

- **Third-party insurance** covers your liability to **other people** injured / killed / property-damaged. Unlimited liability for personal injury / death; limited for property damage.
- **Comprehensive insurance** additionally covers damage to **your own vehicle**.

For motor accident claims, the **insurer of the offending vehicle** is impleaded as a party at MACT. Under MV Act s.149, the insurer must defend the claim, **subject to limited statutory defences** — and the insurer is liable to pay the award (with right of recovery from the insured in defined circumstances, e.g. driving without a valid licence under *National Insurance Co. v. Swaran Singh* (2004)).

### What if the offending vehicle is uninsured?

If the offending vehicle was uninsured or untraceable (hit-and-run), you have the **Solatium Fund** (now subsumed into the **Motor Vehicles Accident Fund** introduced by the 2019 amendment, MV Act s.164B). Compensation under this Fund:

- **₹2 lakh for death**
- **₹50,000 for grievous injury**

Apply through the prescribed process at the MACT or the District Magistrate's office.

## How to actually file a MACT claim — practical steps

### Step 1: FIR
File an FIR at the nearest police station. The FIR is the foundation document. Without it, claims are very difficult.

### Step 2: Documents to collect
- **FIR copy.**
- **Charge sheet** (when filed by police).
- **Driving licence** of the driver.
- **Registration certificate (RC)** of the offending vehicle.
- **Insurance policy** of the offending vehicle.
- **Medical records** — every bill, prescription, discharge summary.
- **Income proof** of the deceased / injured (salary slips, ITR, bank statement, employer certificate).
- **Death certificate** (for fatal cases).
- **Proof of relationship** with the deceased (legal heir certificate).
- **Photographs of the accident scene and damaged vehicles.**
- **Witness affidavits**, if available.

### Step 3: File the claim petition
Under **Form 54 / state-specific MACT forms**, file the claim petition before the appropriate MACT. The MACT for the place of:
- the accident, OR
- the residence of the claimant, OR
- the office of the offending vehicle insurer's local branch
has jurisdiction.

You don't need court fees in MACT cases beyond a nominal filing fee — this is meant to be accessible.

### Step 4: The proceedings
- Notice to respondents (driver, owner, insurer).
- Written statements filed.
- Issues framed.
- Evidence — your witnesses (medical, eyewitness, family); their witnesses (driver, insurer's investigator).
- Arguments.
- Award.

In practice, MACT proceedings take **2 to 5 years** to conclude. **Lok Adalat** settlements are encouraged and can resolve cases faster — typically in single sittings — though at often somewhat reduced compensation.

### Step 5: Recovery and disbursement
After the MACT awards compensation, the insurer pays into the MACT account; the claimants withdraw subject to MACT directions. For minors and elderly claimants, the MACT often directs the amount to be deposited in a fixed deposit and disbursed only as monthly maintenance.

## Appeals

- **MACT → High Court** under MV Act s.173 within **90 days** (extendable on cause shown).
- **High Court → Supreme Court** by special leave under Article 136.

Insurers frequently appeal awards above ₹20 lakh; victims should be prepared for this and not rely on early disbursement.

## Practical tips

- **Don't accept "settlement" amounts at the hospital from random "claim agents."** These touts charge enormous fees for trivial work and you get a fraction of what MACT would award.
- **Preserve the original vehicle** until photos / inspection are done.
- **Keep every receipt.** Even the auto fare to the hospital is a claimable expense.
- **Act quickly on documentation.** Witness availability and scene preservation deteriorate fast.
- **For the family of a deceased breadwinner**: the **Pranay Sethi multiplier method** typically gives compensation in the range of 50–200 times annual income depending on age and dependants. If a settlement is offered well below that, it's likely too low.

## Free legal aid

- **NALSA toll-free helpline: 15100**
- **State Legal Services Authorities** specifically run MACT clinics.
- **District Legal Services Authority** at every district court — free legal representation is your right under Article 39A.
