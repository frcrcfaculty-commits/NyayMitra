# How to Register Your Marriage in India

A registered marriage gives you a **legally recognised proof** of your marital status. You will need it for: passport / visa applications, joint bank accounts, insurance nominations, property transfers, school admissions for children, claiming spousal benefits, and in any matrimonial dispute.

Indian law gives you **three different routes** depending on your circumstances:

1. **Registration under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955** (for Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, Sikhs).
2. **Registration / solemnisation under the Special Marriage Act, 1954** (for any couple, regardless of religion — and the only route for inter-faith couples who do not convert).
3. **Registration under State Marriage Registration Rules** (compulsory in most states, regardless of which Act the marriage was solemnised under, following the Supreme Court direction in *Seema v. Ashwani Kumar* (2006)).

This page is **legal information**, not legal advice.

## First, the threshold conditions

Both the HMA and SMA require certain conditions to be met. Get these right before anything else.

### Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 — **s.5**

A marriage may be solemnised between any two Hindus if:

- **neither party has a living spouse** at the time of marriage;
- both are capable of giving valid consent — and **not** suffering from a mental disorder of a kind or extent unfit for marriage and procreation, or recurrent attacks of insanity;
- the **bridegroom has completed 21 years** and the **bride 18 years**;
- the parties are **not within prohibited degrees of relationship** unless permitted by custom;
- the parties are **not sapindas** of each other unless permitted by custom.

> The phrase "Hindu" under HMA s.2 includes Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, and Sikhs.

### Special Marriage Act, 1954 — **s.4**

A marriage between any two persons may be solemnised under SMA if:

- neither party has a living spouse;
- neither is incapable of giving valid consent due to unsoundness of mind;
- neither is suffering from such a mental disorder, or insanity, or epilepsy that makes them unfit for marriage;
- the **bridegroom has completed 21 years** and the **bride 18 years**;
- the parties are not within the degrees of prohibited relationship in the **First Schedule of the SMA**, unless custom governing one of them permits it.

The SMA applies to **all Indians regardless of religion**, including inter-faith couples without conversion. It is also the recommended route for couples who married abroad and want recognition under Indian law.

## Route 1: Registration of a Hindu marriage already solemnised — HMA s.8

If you have already had a Hindu marriage ceremony (with the rites of *saptapadi* — seven steps — under HMA s.7, in the case of customary rites), you only need to **register** it.

**HMA s.8** authorises State Governments to make rules for registration. Each state has its own Rules (e.g. Maharashtra Hindu Marriage Registration Rules; Delhi Compulsory Registration of Marriage Order, 2014).

### What you need

- **Proof of age** of both parties (Aadhaar, PAN, passport, school leaving certificate, birth certificate).
- **Proof of address** of both parties.
- **Marriage invitation card and / or proof of solemnisation** (priest's certificate, photographs from the ceremony).
- **Affidavit** by both parties stating place and date of marriage, marital status before marriage, nationality, and that they meet the conditions of HMA s.5.
- **Two witnesses** with their identity proof.
- **Photographs** of both parties.
- **Application form** prescribed by the State Rules.
- **Fee** — varies by state, typically Rs. 100–500.

### Where to file

The **Sub-Registrar of Marriages** of the area where the marriage was solemnised, **or** where either party has resided for at least 6 months. For most states, you can apply online through the State Government's marriage registration portal (e.g. `igrmaharashtra.gov.in` for Maharashtra, `edistrict.delhigovt.nic.in` for Delhi).

### Timeline

- **Tatkal** registration (where available): 1–7 days.
- **Normal** registration: 15–30 days from filing.

The Registrar issues a **Marriage Certificate** under the State Rules — this is the document you will use everywhere.

## Route 2: Solemnisation **and** registration under the Special Marriage Act

If you have not yet had a religious ceremony, or you are an inter-faith couple, or you want a purely civil marriage, the SMA is your route. The marriage is **solemnised** by the Marriage Officer — there is no requirement of any religious ceremony.

### Step 1: Notice of intended marriage — SMA s.5
Both parties give **written notice** in the prescribed form to the **Marriage Officer** of the district where **at least one** of you has resided for **not less than 30 days** immediately before the notice.

### Step 2: 30-day publication — SMA s.6 and s.7
The Marriage Officer publishes the notice by affixing it on a notice board at his office, **for 30 days**. During this period, **any person may file an objection** under s.7 if the marriage would contravene the conditions in s.4.

> This 30-day notice period is the SMA's most criticised provision. Critics, including the Law Commission of India (242nd Report) and several PILs, argue it exposes inter-faith couples to harassment by family members or vigilante groups. The Supreme Court in *Safiya Sultana v. State of UP* (2021, Allahabad HC) held that the publication is **directory, not mandatory** if both parties so request — but practice varies by state. Discuss with your lawyer if privacy is a concern.

### Step 3: Objection process — s.7
If an objection is filed, the Marriage Officer must inquire into it and decide within **30 days**. If the objection is upheld, you can appeal to the District Court within 30 days. If no objection is filed, or it is rejected, the marriage can be solemnised.

### Step 4: Declaration — s.11
On the date of solemnisation, both parties and three witnesses sign a declaration in the form in the **Third Schedule** of the SMA.

### Step 5: Solemnisation — s.12
The marriage is solemnised at the office of the Marriage Officer, or any other place within reasonable distance, in the form chosen by the parties. Each party says, in any language understood by the parties: *"I, A, take thee, B, to be my lawful wife / husband."*

### Step 6: Certificate — s.13
The Marriage Officer enters the **Marriage Certificate** in the Marriage Certificate Book in the Fourth Schedule format. **This certificate, on being signed by the parties and the three witnesses, is conclusive evidence of the marriage** under the Act.

### Documents you need

- **Application** in the prescribed form.
- **Proof of age and identity** of both parties.
- **Proof of residence** of at least one party in the district for 30 days before the notice.
- **Affidavits** by both parties stating marital status, mental capacity, and that they are not within prohibited degrees.
- **Three witnesses** (with ID).
- **Photographs**.
- If either party is a foreign national: a **No Objection Certificate** from the embassy of that country and a valid visa.
- If either party was previously married: divorce decree or death certificate of the former spouse.

### Fees

Vary by state. Typically Rs. 100–1,000 for the application, plus a similar amount for the certificate.

## Route 3: Registration of a marriage solemnised under any other law — SMA Chapter III (s.15–s.18)

If your marriage was already solemnised under any law (HMA, Christian marriage under the Indian Christian Marriage Act 1872, Muslim marriage, Parsi marriage, etc.), you can **also** register it under the SMA in addition. This gives you the SMA-based legal regime for divorce and succession.

**Both** parties must:
- have been married under another law;
- have **resided together** since the marriage;
- have completed **21 years** at the time of registration;
- not be in a prohibited degree of relationship;
- have lived in the district of the Marriage Officer for at least **30 days** immediately before applying.

A **30-day notice + objection** process applies, similar to Route 2.

After registration under SMA, the marriage is governed by SMA for succession, divorce, etc. — so this has consequences. Get advice before choosing this route over plain State-Rules registration.

## Inter-faith marriages — practical notes

- The **only** legal mechanism for an inter-faith marriage **without conversion** is the Special Marriage Act.
- Some states have enacted **"anti-conversion"** laws (e.g. Uttar Pradesh Prohibition of Unlawful Conversion of Religion Act, 2021; Madhya Pradesh Freedom of Religion Act, 2021; Uttarakhand, Himachal, Karnataka, etc.). These laws regulate religious conversion *for the purpose of marriage* and impose notice / inquiry requirements that go beyond the SMA. **Get specific legal advice if your state has such a law and either party intends to convert.**
- The **Allahabad High Court** in *Safiya Sultana* (2021) held that publication of the notice under SMA s.6 is at the option of the parties, not mandatory. A few other states have followed this.

## The compulsory registration mandate

The Supreme Court in *Seema v. Ashwani Kumar* (2006) directed all State Governments to enact **compulsory marriage registration** rules covering marriages of all religions. Most states have done so. Maharashtra's **Regulation of Marriages Bureaus and Registration of Marriages Act, 1998**, for example, applies to all marriages in the state regardless of religion.

So in practice you may need **both**:
1. Solemnisation under your personal law / SMA, **and**
2. Registration under your State's compulsory registration rules.

The State certificate is what most government bodies actually ask for.

## Privacy and DPDP considerations

The SMA's 30-day public notice publishes your **personal information** (names, ages, addresses) on a public notice board. The interaction between this publication requirement and the **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** (which restricts processing of personal data without consent or specified legitimate uses) has not yet been judicially settled. If publication exposes you to harm, you may invoke the *Safiya Sultana* line of authority and request the Marriage Officer to dispense with public publication.

## What if there is a problem?

- **Marriage Officer / Registrar refuses to register:** appeal to the District Registrar / District Court within 30 days (procedure varies by state).
- **Objection filed under SMA s.7 you believe is mala fide:** present evidence to the Marriage Officer; appeal to the District Court if upheld.
- **Pressure or threats from family / community:** seek police protection. The Supreme Court in *Lata Singh v. State of UP* (2006) and *Shakti Vahini v. Union of India* (2018) directed protection mechanisms for inter-caste and inter-faith couples.
