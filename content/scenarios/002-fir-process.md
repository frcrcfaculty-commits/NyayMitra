# How to File an FIR (First Information Report)

## What is an FIR?

An FIR (First Information Report) is a written document prepared by police when they receive information about the commission of a **cognizable offense**. It is the first step in the criminal justice process.

## Your Right to File an FIR

Under **Section 154 of CrPC** (now **Section 173 of BNSS, 2023**):

- Police **must** register an FIR for cognizable offenses
- Refusal to register an FIR is a **punishable offense**
- You can file an FIR at **any police station** (Zero FIR concept)
- An FIR can be filed by the victim or any person who knows about the offense

## Step-by-Step Process

### Step 1: Go to the Police Station
- Visit the nearest police station
- You have the right to file a **Zero FIR** at any police station, regardless of jurisdiction

### Step 2: Provide Information
- Describe the incident clearly: **what, when, where, who**
- The information can be given **orally or in writing**
- If given orally, the police officer must write it down and read it back to you

### Step 3: Verify and Sign
- Read the FIR carefully before signing
- You have the right to get a **free copy** of the FIR
- Note down the FIR number for future reference

### Step 4: Get Your Copy
- Under the law, you are entitled to a **free copy of the FIR immediately**
- If the police refuse, this is a violation of your rights

## If Police Refuse to File an FIR

You have several options:

1. **Written complaint to SP/DSP**: Send a registered letter to the Superintendent of Police
2. **Approach the Magistrate**: Under Section 156(3) CrPC / Section 175(3) BNSS, you can approach a Judicial Magistrate who can direct the police to register the FIR
3. **Online FIR**: Many states now allow filing FIRs online through their state police websites
4. **Email complaint**: Send a complaint via email to the concerned police authority
5. **File a complaint with State Human Rights Commission**

## Important Points

- **Time limit**: There is generally no time limit for filing an FIR, but filing it early strengthens your case
- **Language**: The FIR should be in a language you understand. You can request translation
- **Women complainants**: Between 6 PM and 6 AM, a woman complainant's statement should be recorded at her residence
- **SC/ST cases**: Under the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, the FIR must be registered immediately

## Relevant Laws

- Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), 1973 — Section 154
- Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023 — Section 173
- Lalita Kumari v. Govt. of UP (2014) — Supreme Court ruling making FIR registration mandatory
